# Task: Handle a Sales Objection

Recipe for writing a reply to an outbound objection — concise, non-pushy, leaves the door open without burning the relationship.

## When to use this

User received an objection in reply to outbound (cold email, LinkedIn) and asks how to respond — "they said not interested", "they want more info", "wrong person", etc.

## Inputs required

- Original message you sent
- The objection (paste the exact reply)
- ICP and offer context

## The 6 objection types — handle each differently

### "Not interested"
- Don't push. Acknowledge + leave door open.
- Format: 1 sentence validating their call + 1 sentence planting a seed for later
- Mark contact as "contacted, not now". No follow-up after this reply.

### "We already have a solution / vendor"
- Don't attack the incumbent (reads as defensive)
- Ask one question that reveals whether their current solution is actually working
- If they engage → qualify further. If silent → close it.

### "Not the right time / too busy"
- Often genuine. Respect it.
- Offer a specific future date: "Would [month] make more sense?"
- If yes → schedule the follow-up. If no → close it.

### "Send me more information"
- Often a polite brush-off, sometimes genuine
- DON'T send a wall of text or 12-slide deck
- Send: 1 short paragraph + 1 link (case study / landing page / Loom)
- End with one qualifying question — they have to engage to get the value

### "Not the right person"
- Ask for the right person by name or role
- Keep it to one sentence — no explanation
- If they refer → send a short intro-style message to the new contact referencing who sent you

### "We're not doing anything in this space right now"
- Treat like a timing objection — plant a flag
- Acknowledge + ask: "what would need to change for this to become a priority?"

## Process

1. Identify the objection type from the 6 above
2. Draft reply per the type's guidance
3. Keep total reply under 80 words — shorter is better for objections
4. End with: a question, a specific future date offer, or a graceful close
5. **Never** end with "let me know if you change your mind" (passive, no ask)

## Output format

```
Objection: [type]
Their reply: "[exact text]"

RESPONSE
---
[drafted reply]
---

Tone check: [non-pushy / direct / warm]
Word count: [n]
Next action: [mark not-interested / schedule follow-up / send resource / wait for reply]
```

## Pass criteria

- Word count < 80
- Reply ends in one of: question, specific date, or graceful close (NOT a passive "let me know")
- No defensiveness, no apology, no "I know you're busy but..."
- Tone matches type (not interested = warm + brief; wrong person = single sentence)

## Common failures

- **Trying to override the objection** — the goal is NOT to reverse "no", it's to leave the door open without being annoying
- **Multiple replies after objection** — one reply per objection, max. If they don't respond, close.
- **"Not interested" with new pitch** — never. Acknowledge + soft seed only.
- **Wall of text on "send more info"** — defeats the purpose of the objection-as-brush-off filter
