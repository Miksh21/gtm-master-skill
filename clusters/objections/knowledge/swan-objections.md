### Call scorecards
- Start a scorecard with the 10-15 most important behaviours, taken from what top reps actually do on real calls and built with rep + manager input; every item is an observable behaviour scored 1-5 ("got them to agree they have a problem", never "good discovery"); grow it only once the scoring habit exists. [kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards]
- Group items by call section (opener / body / wrap-up; discovery / pitch / close / navigation) so a low score localises the problem; practice drills ONLY the lowest-scoring section, one role play per rep per week minimum. [kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards]
- A prospecting call is not a qualifying call: opening with qualifying questions (size, title, stack) kills the meeting rate; ask permission for a couple of questions, close on the problems just discovered, book within three days and get the invite accepted on the spot. [kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards]
- Closing: first ask "what problem did they agree they have?"; confirm they are sold before showing pricing; ask for the money or the concrete next step instead of waiting; a scored miss on champion navigation (champion personally sold, decision process mapped) is the signal to go back to the champion BEFORE the decision call. [kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards]
- SDRs and AEs can score every call (reps self-score, managers sample); transcript scoring cites the quoted moment per item or marks it unobserved, never invented. [kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards]

### Playbook structure
- Playbook order People, Prospect, Problem, Process, Product (people execute the plays). The People section holds: targets as re-derivable math ($100K/quarter = $10K x 10 deals at 25% close), promotion AND termination criteria known from day one, standards of excellence defined with real examples and signed, personal goals, and stories/records. [kevin-kd-dorsey/sales-playbook-foundations]
- Build the other sections from real calls, deals and rep language, keep the leader heavily involved, assign an owner and review cadence before shipping; acceptance test: could a rep who joins after the leader has left succeed from this document alone? [kevin-kd-dorsey/sales-playbook-foundations]

### Battlecards
- Six sections, under ~600 words, date-stamped: one-line description; where they win (3-5 genuine strengths, since a card pretending they have none goes unused); where they lose (evidenced); objection -> response pairs under 30 words; win conditions; where to push in a deal. [ido-goldberg/battlecard]
- Strengths and weaknesses need real evidence or are left out; rumoured weaknesses are labelled with their source ("reported in G2 reviews"); gather cheap signals first (homepage, pricing headline, customers page, LinkedIn cadence and hiring, traffic magnitude, funding). [ido-goldberg/battlecard]
- Ghost-competitor check: under ~1,000 monthly visitors and no LinkedIn presence = question whether it deserves a card; existing notes are updated, not recreated; the card is saved to shared competitive knowledge, never left in chat. [ido-goldberg/battlecard]
