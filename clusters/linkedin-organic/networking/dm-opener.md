# LinkedIn DM Opener

The DM after the accept is the moment most people lose the connection. This skill keeps the conversation alive without pitching.

## When to trigger

The user says "they accepted my request, now what ?", "write the follow-up DM", "first message after connection", "I always pitch too early, fix this".

## Inputs to ask for

1. The new connection's name, role, company.
2. Why the user connected (recall the connection request context).
3. The user's positioning.
4. The desired outcome :
   - **Build relationship** (default, slow burn, no agenda).
   - **Discovery call** (only if there is a clear, mutual reason).
   - **Specific ask** (intro, advice, feedback).
5. Any recent activity from the connection (a post, a launch, a job change) you can reference.

## The 3 rules of a great DM opener

1. **Reference something specific**. A post they wrote, a project they shared, a context you both know. Generic openers get archived.
2. **Give before you take**. Offer a thought, a resource, a useful question, BEFORE asking for anything.
3. **Make it easy to reply**. End with a low-friction question (yes/no, A or B, "what was the toughest part of X ?").

## The 3 patterns by goal

### Build relationship (default)
- Thank them for accepting (1 line, no flattery).
- Reference the context that triggered the request (their post, the event, the mutual contact).
- Offer one thought or ask one question they will enjoy answering.
- No CTA. The reply IS the goal.

### Discovery call
- Only use this if you have actual reason to believe there is mutual interest.
- Reference the context.
- Name the specific overlap ("we both work with X, and I have seen you do Y").
- Suggest a 15-minute call with a clear agenda. Always include "no pitch, just curious".

### Specific ask
- Reference the context.
- State the ask clearly and explain why you are asking THEM specifically.
- Make it small. A 2-minute reply > a 30-minute call.
- Offer something in return.

## Output format

```
GOAL : [build / discovery / specific ask]

OPTION 1 (RECOMMENDED)
"[message, 3 to 6 sentences, max 600 characters]"
Reply odds : [Low / Medium / High] | Why : [one-liner]

OPTION 2
"[variant]"

OPTION 3
"[variant]"

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not paste a calendar link in DM 1.
- Do not auto-DM via tools that scream "you are user #4732".
- Do not start with "Hope you are doing well !". It is the email equivalent of telephone hold music.
```

## Rules

- 600 characters max. Shorter usually wins.
- No links in DM 1 (unless directly requested in conversation).
- Mirror the language they use on their profile.
- If they are clearly senior, lead with respect for their time : "30 seconds of context, then a low-stakes question".
- If they are a peer, lead with warmth and specificity.
- Never use "I am reaching out to..." It is the universal red flag.
