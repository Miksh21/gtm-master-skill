# Ad Copywriting: Formulas, Visual Rules, TLA Writing

## Rule #1: Write From the Customer's Mouth

Brand voice describes features ("real-time visibility across all business units"); customer voice describes the transformation ("I used to spend 6-8 hours a week on reporting, now it takes 15 minutes"). Customer voice wins every time. Gather at least 5 real customer quotes before writing any ad copy.

**Voice-of-customer sources, ranked:** Tier 1 direct: G2/Capterra/TrustRadius reviews (before/after states), sales call transcripts (unfiltered problem language), website testimonials/case studies (numbers), support tickets. Tier 2 in the wild: Reddit threads, ICP LinkedIn posts, conference/webinar transcripts. Tier 3 competitive: competitor G2 reviews (what users wish it did), competitor ad copy via ad libraries. Extract: pain phrases ("drowning in spreadsheets"), outcome phrases ("saved me hours"), specific numbers ("6-8 hours to 15 minutes"), emotional words (stress, overwhelm).

## The Headline IS the Creative

The #1 visual pattern across top B2B ads on LinkedIn and Meta: no stock photos; the headline text is the main graphic. Bold chunky sans-serif, one sentence max, 50-70% of image area, readable at thumbnail size, solid color background. Color distribution among top ads: dark backgrounds ~40%, bold bright (neon green, hot pink, yellow) ~35%, light ~15-20%; bright pops hardest against LinkedIn's white feed. On the image: one headline, small logo, optional embedded CTA button, one accent color. Never: multiple messages, stock photography, feature lists, small text.

**What wins visually (2026 analysis of 463+ top image ads):** specific offers (FREE / $ / deadline) in 65% of top vs 10% of low performers; real people with names 47% (stock photos mark 35% of LOW performers); high-contrast CTA button 59% vs 20%; humor/pattern-interrupt 35% vs 2%; dark background + neon text in 35% of ads at >= 2% CTR. Avoid: text-heavy layouts, logo-as-hero, UI-screenshot dumps, generic minimalism, vague "Learn More".

## Six Headline Formulas

| Formula | Structure | Best at | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeling | [Positive emotion], not [negative] | TOF | "Insights, not anxiety." |
| Conversation | 2-4 line lived dialogue, punchline on the pain, product never named | TOF | "Board: 'What's our current position?' / You: 'Give me a day to pull the numbers.'" |
| Contrast | No more X. No more Y. Just Z. / old -> new | MOF | "6-8 hours on reporting -> 15 minutes." |
| Shame | Status quo framed as outdated, light and observational | MOF | "It's 2026. Why is your forecast still in a spreadsheet?" |
| Stat interrupt | One dramatic specific number AS the visual, minimal context under it | Any | "6-8hrs -> 15min" giant, "20 fewer days to close." |
| Pain + outcome | [Pain verb] the [old thing], [outcome verb] [result], under 10 words | MOF-BOF | "Ditch the spreadsheets, scale faster." |

Strong verbs: ditch, cut, stop, kill, end / get, gain, unlock-free alternatives: see, build. Stats must be specific and verifiable; round numbers feel invented.

**Process:** 1) gather VOC (min 5 quotes). 2) one idea per ad; two ideas = two ads. 3) pick formula by idea + stage. 4) write the image text FIRST; it must stop the scroll standalone. 5) body copy expands, verified brand language, social proof, one clear next step. 6) headline field supports, never repeats the image (customer name, outcome stat, or CTA). 7) audit: factual accuracy, tone, structure, design density, audience fit.

Common failures: brand-marketing language, feature headlines, multiple ideas on one ad, generic pain questions ("Struggling with data management?" vs "Your board asked for a forecast. How long did it take?"), vague outcomes, photo-first design.

## The 7-Step Meta Body Copy Formula

1 Direct offer ("Stop losing pipeline to dirty CRM data.") 2 Pain ("Your reps spend 15 hours/week on manual data entry.") 3 Solution 4 Pain explained (consequences) 5 Solution explained (benefits) 6 Social proof ("'We went from 3 days to 30 seconds.' VP Sales, mid-market SaaS") 7 CTA with motivation ("Calculate how much pipeline you're leaking."). Rules: sell the click, not the product; call out who it is for (mosquito repellent); name the specific problem, not the category; time-bound element when possible ("in 60 days"); first 2-3 lines must work before "See More"; compress the structure into 2-3 lines for short-form; double line breaks between paragraphs via API.

## Writing Thought Leader Ads (TLAs)

A TLA runs under a real person's name and reads as an organic post; the moment it reads like an ad, it is dead. Benchmarks from a 161,256-ad, $5.5M-spend analysis: TLA median CTR 2.68%, effective cost per landing-page click $3.06, roughly 4x cheaper per LP click than image ads ($13.23). Under-investing in TLAs is usually the single biggest fix in a LinkedIn account.

**Canonical rules (top vs bottom performer patterns):**
- 1st person "I" voice: 65% of top performers vs 30% of bottom. Corporate "we": 0% of top.
- Link in the bottom 25% of the text: 75% of top. Mid-text links: 40% of bottom.
- 1,000-1,500 characters optimal (55% of top); under 500 delivers no value, over 2,000 loses the reader. Engagement-variant: 600-1,000.
- Pain-point hooks ("I've seen so many...") win. Webinar CTAs: 0% of top 20, 0.07-0.08% LP CTR. Never use them.
- 1-2 sentence paragraphs, line breaks everywhere (100% of top), 1-3 functional emojis max, 0-2 hashtags.
- CTA is a demo or trial link only, pull not push ("Want to find out more? Head to [link]", "You can try it free for [X] days"); never blog posts, downloads, "book a consultation", "limited spots", "comment X to get Y".
- Never mention or quote competitors. Describe the problem, not who else fails at it.
- Social proof with specific metrics appears in 45% of top TLAs ("$650,000 in pipeline in 90 days", "$12 in pipeline per $ spent").

**Required inputs before writing, never fabricate:** who posts (name, position; voice and stories must be theirs), real numbers/mistakes/client stories for the use case (a metric slightly uncomfortable to share performs), the CTA destination URL. If the author has no real specifics, write the draft with [bracketed placeholders] and flag it weaker; invented specifics under a real name are a liability.

**Hooks (90% of readers see only the first 2-3 lines):** challenge an assumption; bold contrarian statement ("[Popular tactic] is an oxymoron"); results-first ("[UPDATE: number, outcome, timeframe]"); personal narrative with credible detail ("I've seen so many businesses still running operations on Excel spreadsheets. It works, until it doesn't." = 3.42% LP CTR); shared pain ("Every [title] I talk to says the same thing."). Avoid: generic openers ("Running an agency is a constant balancing act" = 0.04% LP CTR), starting with the company/product name, opening questions, future-vision hooks.

**Structure arc:** TENSION -> SPECIFICITY (concrete example/number/story) -> INSIGHT (the reframe) -> RESOLUTION (the better way) -> PROOF -> SOFT CTA + link. Character budget: hook 50-100, backstory 300-500, problem detail 200-300, solution + 3-4 benefit checkmarks 300-400, social proof 100-200, CTA 50-100. Formats that work: what/why/how, honest results (include what did not work), "I made this mistake", contrarian take (attack the tactic, not people), named framework.

**Pre-delivery checklist:** first line creates tension or a bold specific claim; 1st person in the author's plausible voice; at least one real number or concrete example supplied by the author; no sentence over 25 words; 1,000-1,500 chars; product appears as resolution, not centerpiece; exactly one pull CTA in the bottom 25%; reads aloud like a person; every sentence survives "could I delete this?".

## 1:1 ABM Ad Copy (three layers, designed together)

For personalized single-account ads, copy spans creative (image), commentary (feed text), landing page. Rules:
- The ad's only job is ONE CLICK to the page. Reverse-engineer every line from "what makes this person click", not from the meeting.
- Never put landing-page-internal language (offer names, named frameworks, "30-day custom proposal") in the ad; a cold reader has not seen the page. Plain self-evident CTA: "see the playbook", "see the {N} plays".
- Speak TO the company's own employees, second person always ("your buyers", "what you sell"); third person ("plays we built for them") is wrong because targeting = employers of that company.
- Read all three layers before writing. Never invent a play, number, or claim not on the landing page. Inconsistency between ad and page: FLAG it, do not smooth it over.
- Creative structure: headline addresses the company by name + what this one play does ("{Company}: know the moment a company leaves {Competitor 1} or {Competitor 2}."); subheadline establishes it is one of several ("We built this play and {N-1} more into a playbook for {Company}."); CTA points to the full set. The commentary may only reference what the creative visibly establishes, never narrates the ad, never repeats the headline.
- Cohesion without redundancy: creative = one play hook + promise of more; commentary = why it matters + nudge; page = substance + booking.

## Carousel and Format Micro-Rules

Carousel: 5-7 cards; card 1 carries ~76% of impressions, so the cover must carry a strong statement + promise; one message per card; product connection on the penultimate card; CTA on the final card. LinkedIn text ads: 25-char headline, 75-char description. Video intro text 600 chars, headline 200 chars. Personalization macros where supported: %FIRSTNAME%, %COMPANYNAME%, %INDUSTRY%, %JOBTITLE%.

## Image-Ad Design Patterns (mockup shorthand)

Default to a rich style: gradient background, one glass element, 1-2 subtle shapes, shadows on floating elements. Pick the pattern by the ad's job: stat highlight with pill; product screenshot + headline + CTA (mockup fills bottom 50-60%); card-on-dark for downloadables; dark playbook promo (ALL-CAPS + chapter pills); before -> after split (red tints -> green tints); contrarian giant-headline; social proof logo bar (huge industry keyword + 3-4 logos); data-viz 3-act (setup -> chart -> conclusion); question hook + bold answer; testimonial + review badge + metric pills; tool-consolidation grid ("Replace them all with one."); case-study speech bubble with a massive stat. Photo rules: pre-crop to the container's aspect ratio (never clip a head), fixed-size container with object-fit cover, prefer contained slots (avatar circle, right column) over full-bleed, overlays capped at ~55% opacity.
