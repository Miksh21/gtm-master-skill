# Task: Write a PS Line

Recipe for writing a P.S. line on a cold email — the most-read part of a long email and the last thing seen in a short one.

## When to use this

User asks for a PS line, wants to add a pattern interrupt to an email, or wants to improve the bottom of a sequence step.

## Inputs required

- **Email body** the PS line will be added to
- **ICP and persona** context
- **Signal and hypothesis** the email is built around
- **Goal** the PS line should serve (pick from list below)

## The 5 PS line goals — pick ONE per email

| Goal | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Social proof** | Persona is risk-averse or skeptical of outcomes | "P.S. We ran this for a HubSpot agency similar to yours — 14 meetings booked in the first 6 weeks." |
| **Relevance signal** | First touch where you need to prove research, not pitch | "P.S. Saw your team is hiring 3 more SDRs this quarter — that's exactly when this matters." |
| **Urgency / timing** | Real, calendar-driven reason now is the right moment | "P.S. Most teams kick this off before EOQ when budgets reset." |
| **Curiosity** | Step 2 or 3 where you need a NEW hook | "P.S. There's a second angle here that's actually weirder than the first — happy to share if you're curious." |
| **De-risk** | Persona has commitment anxiety (ATL, gatekeepers, multi-stakeholder buy) | "P.S. Not pitching — just want to know if this is a current priority." |

## Rules (non-negotiable)

- **1-2 sentences max** — anything longer reads as a second email
- **Never duplicate the body** — the PS adds new info or a new angle
- **Self-contained** — someone who only reads the PS should still get the point
- **No fake urgency** — banned: "limited spots", "only reaching out to 5 companies", "exclusive offer"
- **Make them think "wait, that's interesting"** — not "okay, another sales email"

## Process

1. Read the email body. Identify what it argues and what it's missing.
2. Pick the goal that complements (not repeats) the body:
   - Body already has a stat → don't pick Social Proof
   - Body already references their company specifically → don't pick Relevance Signal
   - Step 1 → favor Relevance Signal or De-risk
   - Step 2/3 → favor Curiosity or Social Proof
3. Draft 3 PS line options, each from a DIFFERENT goal category
4. Score each:
   - **Relevant** (0-2) — does it map to this persona + signal?
   - **Curiosity** (0-2) — does it create a "tell me more" feeling?
   - **Not salesy** (0-2) — would a non-salesperson write this?
   - Max: 6
5. Rank, recommend a default with a one-line reason

## Output format

```
PS lines for: [step] — [hypothesis]
Email summary: [one line on what the email argues]
Best-fit goal: [why this goal complements the body]

OPTION 1 — [goal]
P.S. [text]
Score: [n/6] | [why it works]

OPTION 2 — [goal]
P.S. [text]
Score: [n/6] | [why it works]

OPTION 3 — [goal]
P.S. [text]
Score: [n/6] | [why it works]

Recommended: Option [n] — [one-line reason]
```

## Pass criteria

- All 3 options are 1-2 sentences
- 3 different goals represented
- Top option scores ≥ 4/6
- No banned phrases ("limited", "exclusive", "only reaching out to X")
- PS does not repeat any line in the body

## Common failures

- **PS is the strongest line in the email** → the body needs a rewrite, not a stronger PS
- **Generic social proof** ("we work with great companies") → swap to specific outcome with a number
- **Curiosity that's vague** ("there's something interesting") → be specific enough that it makes them ask, not roll their eyes
- **Stacking PS lines** ("P.S." then "P.P.S.") → never. One PS only.

## What to do after

- Run the PS through `clusters/cold-email/tasks/stress-test-sequence.md` separately — it's often the highest-read part of the email
- If PS is on Step 1 and uses Social Proof, save room on Step 2 for a Curiosity PS (rotate goals across the sequence)
