# Task: Reframe the Offer (Mafia Offer)

Rephrase what a business already sells into an offer the prospect feels **chosen by** rather
than pitched at. The product does not change; the framing does. Runs BEFORE any copy is
written — if the offer is weak, no subject line saves it.

> Source: https://github.com/Louis-ColdIQ/mafia-offer (Louis van Wyk, ColdIQ, MIT). Installed
> at Jan's direct request 2026-08-24. Adapted to gtm-master conventions; flags below are ours.

## When to use this

User says "mafia offer", "reframe this offer", "make this offer stronger", "rewrite my cold
email offer", "my reply rate is low", "this pitch sounds like everyone else" — or a
`write-first-email` run reveals there is nothing compelling to say. Chain: this task → then
`clusters/cold-email/tasks/write-first-email.md`, never the other way round.

**Core principle:** the offer does most of the work and the copy just delivers it. A strong
offer with average copy beats a weak offer with perfect copy every time.

## Inputs required

Ask for these four. Do not proceed on guesses, and ask again if an answer is vague.

1. **What do they actually sell?** The literal product or service, plainly.
2. **Who are they selling it to?** Title, company type, roughly how many are on the list.
3. **What does the current email say?** Paste it if one exists. If not, write the obvious
   version yourself so there is something to reframe against.
4. **What can they give away for free without it hurting?** A list, an audit, a teardown, a
   recording, a template. This is usually where the reframe comes from.

If they cannot answer 4, that is the finding. Say so. Most weak offers are weak because there
is nothing to give.

## The reframe

Work through these in order. Stop at the first one that fits, do not stack them.

| Move | What it does | Reads like |
|---|---|---|
| **Make them the prize** | Flip who is being chosen | "Several influencers want to collaborate with you" |
| **Imply exclusivity** | One per area, one per category | "Looking for our partner in {{city}}" |
| **Reframe the transaction** | Sell it as an opportunity, not a purchase | "My producer wants to discuss featuring you" |
| **Lead with the asset** | The thing IS the email, no pitch needed | "I pulled 400 founders hiring SDRs. Want the file?" |
| **Bound it in time** | Free, narrow, and it expires | "Audit back to you by Friday. No call, no charge" |

Two rules that survive every version of this:

- **Never put the price in the email.** It kills curiosity and turns the prospect into a
  wallet. Price gets closed on the call.
- **The smaller the ask, the higher the reply rate.** A call is a big ask. A yes-or-no
  question is a small one.

### ⚠️ House flags (ours, not the source's)

- **Truthful framing only.** The source's worked examples 02 and 03 cross into implied
  deception (a paid ad framed as an editorial pick; "not expecting payment" as scarcity
  theater). The reframe must describe something that is literally true of the sender's
  business. If the exclusivity, the producer, or the eager influencers do not actually exist,
  the move is off the table.
- **Talent'em: "Lead with the asset" is a value-based CTA and is HARD-BANNED** in Talent'em
  copy (no ukázka/sample/shortlist offers; call/chat CTAs only — TB-A/B/C are the one
  deliberate exception). Use the other four moves for Talent'em.
- **Layering with canon:** this task fixes WHAT is offered. `write-first-email` still leads
  sentence 1 with the signal, keeps one soft CTA, and holds the word counts. The reframe
  becomes the offer/CTA half of that skeleton, not a replacement for it.

## The checklist

The offer is not ready until all five are true. Check them explicitly and name any that fail.

1. **Specific outcome plus a timeline.** Not "more meetings". X in Y days. Numbers force clarity.
2. **Clear steps to get there.** They can picture how it happens. No black box.
3. **Social proof.** Does not have to be an exact client. Years, a project, a relevant result.
4. **Low commitment ask.** Free, fast, no call. This is the biggest single lever.
5. **Relevance to them.** Built for their situation, not blasted at a list.

## The frame

Same offer, three ways to say it. Strongest first.

- **Insider.** "Noticed [specific thing] about your business, built a custom approach for it."
- **Expert.** "We have run N campaigns and know which plays work for your industry."
- **Commodity.** "We do cold email for B2B." Never ship this one.

## Output

Return exactly this, nothing else:

```
THE OBVIOUS PITCH
<the version most people would send>

WHY IT DIES
<three bullets>

THE REFRAME
<one or two sentences, the actual email opener>

WHY IT WORKS
<three bullets>

CHECKLIST
<all five, each passed or failed with one line of reasoning>

FRAME USED
<insider / expert, and why>
```

## The test before you hand it over

Read the reframe out loud. If a VP of Sales hearing it would not immediately say "tell me
more", it is a regular offer with good framing, not a mafia offer. Go back to the reframe
table.

## Worked examples (from the source — pattern to match, not copy to lift)

Same product, same list, same sender in each case. Only the framing moved.

**01 · Commercial solar installer** (move: imply exclusivity)
- Obvious: "we install commercial solar… clients save 20-30%… want a 15-min call?" — sounds
  like every solar pitch, big ask, zero value upfront.
- Reframe: *"We are looking for our commercial solar partner in {{city}}. Partners get 20%
  off."* — one per area, "partner" beats "customer", the saving is baked into the hook.

**02 · Business TV show selling $25K feature segments** (move: reframe the transaction)
- Obvious: "we have paid feature spots for $25K, want to chat?" — media-buy pitch, price
  upfront kills curiosity.
- Reframe: *"I am the Programming Director… my Senior Producer wants to discuss featuring
  {{companyName}} in an upcoming episode."* — the prospect feels chosen; price closes on the
  call. ⚠️ Only usable if the editorial interest is real — see house flags.

**03 · Influencer platform for hospitality brands** (move: make them the prize)
- Obvious: "we run an influencer platform, quick call?" — generic SaaS pitch.
- Reframe: *"Several lifestyle and travel influencers want to collaborate with
  {{companyName}}. They are not expecting payment, just memorable experiences to promote."*
  — the prospect is the prize, the offer IS the email. ⚠️ Only usable if those influencers
  exist and the payment claim is true — see house flags.

**The pattern** — all three do the same three things: obviously valuable just from reading
it; near-zero risk to engage; the prospect feels chosen or invited, not pitched. If a
reframe does not do all three, it is not there yet.
