## 1. Description is not an offer
- "We build scrapers / run cold email / do LinkedIn content" = a description. An offer = what you will do for a SPECIFIC person + why they should trust you; it is what makes a stranger buy. [keller-100m]
- Baseline shape: `<clear outcome> in <time> without <annoying specific thing> + <risk reversal>`. Ex: "our setters book you 5-10 appointments/month in the next 28 days, or you don't pay." This is the easy, commoditised part; everyone's inbox is full of it. [keller-100m]

## 2. Value equation, corrected for strangers
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) / (Time delay × Effort). Keller's two corrections:
- **Bigger dream outcome LOWERS value with strangers.** A stranger has no reason to believe you; the bigger the promise, the less believable. Do not lead by inflating the outcome ("10x revenue", "go viral"). [keller-100m]
- **Worldview alignment is the real lever, not proof of past results.** Show you understand them as well as they understand themselves (insider vocabulary, the problems only they live with). It wraps the whole equation: makes the outcome specific (you know what they actually want), and makes speed and ease believable (you have been deep enough in their world to know how fast it can be done). Case studies and testimonials help, but are secondary. [keller-100m]
  - Mechanism, not adjective: replace "businesses like yours" with the 2-3 facts only their segment knows (wearing too many hats; tariffs breaking the distribution line; for a winery, the specific tax that would be a dream to cut). [keller-100m]
  - Worked rewrite: "We'll make you go viral on LinkedIn" (zero alignment) → "We'll make you the most viral YC founder in this batch in 5 posts, so every sales call feels warm and even Paul Graham wonders how you did it" (niche named, founder-led sales assumed, the real outcome = pipeline feel, not impressions). [keller-100m]
  - The ONLY time to drop plain language: when using words only that market would understand. [keller-100m]
- **Too easy kills believability.** "10x your pipeline with zero effort" reads as fake. **Every bold claim needs a mechanism** (the reason it is possible): fast because of proprietary infrastructure; low effort because done for 100 companies in their exact vertical. Ex: "written in the voice of your last month of LinkedIn posts, so approvals are a thumbs-up in Slack" explains speed AND ease AND proves you already studied them. [keller-100m]
- **Keep effort where it raises likelihood.** A little prospect effort helps when it visibly increases their perceived chance of success (and gives ownership). Remove all other effort. [keller-100m]

## 3. Guarantees
- Generic guarantees backfire: money-back attracts tire kickers; "100% satisfaction" means nothing. Winning guarantees are **specific enough to be believable** ("free if we don't get X impressions or 100k views in 14 days, whichever is higher"). [keller-100m]
- Segment rule: to small businesses a guarantee attracts broke customers; to larger companies it is a simple risk reversal worth testing inside the offer. "We'll work until it's done" is a stupid guarantee; a real money-back is fine if you can stand behind it. Tier B as a standalone offer. [keller-27]
- Copy note (ours): the literal word "guarantee" is on the spam-word list (`copywriting-principles.md`); state the condition ("free if…", "you don't pay unless…") instead of the word.

## 4. Intro offer: the value-equation offer captures demand, the intro offer creates it
- A full value-equation offer only converts EXISTING demand (people already wanting what you sell). A stranger has no demand yet and may not know the problem exists. [keller-100m]
- **Never sell the whole service to a stranger.** Take ONE result, the most specific and believable thing you can deliver, and make it the intro offer; the full service is what they buy after. "Free taste" logic: pick the piece of the service or product that hooks people immediately and deliver it as fast and cheap as possible. [keller-100m]
- Keller's own: "Outbound dream-lead sprint: we contact 5,000 qualified dream decision makers in your market in the next 7 days, free, with messages they'll thank you for. You only sit down with us and approve the offer we send." Specific; the approval effort gives ownership; a slice that proves competence. [keller-100m]
- Build BOTH (value-equation offer for demand capture, intro offer for demand creation) and pipeline does not run dry. Maps onto `reference/three-gtm-plays-by-buyer-state.md`: intro offer = demand-creation play; value-equation offer = capture/conversion. [keller-100m]
- **Stranger-proof = everywhere-proof:** an offer that converts someone with no context, no trust and no intent converts better on every warmer channel (ads, content, referrals, website). Design for cold first. [keller-100m]
- **Narrow the eligibility to raise likelihood:** e.g. the viral-LinkedIn offer only for founders with an existing newsletter funnel to feed. Fewer qualify, the promise becomes believable. Test it. [keller-100m]

### The 5 intro-offer rules (checklist) [keller-100m]
1. Stupid simple.
2. Speaks to an outcome or problem only THEY would know and experience.
3. In their language.
4. Risk reversed by a SPECIFIC guarantee.
5. Effort on their side only where it matters.

## 5. The 27 outbound offers ranked (criterion: can it turn a stranger into a customer?) [keller-27]
Final board as shown on screen. "Add-on" = belongs in the PS, cannot be the offer itself.

| Tier | Offer | Condition / mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Charity donation | Add-on (PS): "pick your charity, I donate for your time". Ranked above gift card ("good karma"). Make it meaningful to the individual. |
| **S** | Gift card | Add-on only, never the offer: frictionless question + "PS, happy to send a $X gift card / buy you a pizza from <local place> for 5 minutes". Ramp-style. Better when the gift is researched and specific to the person. |
| **S** | Free work up front | Almost any service business can do it (free landing page, UGC or ad creatives, cold email copy, a list). Strongest form: permissionless, i.e. the work is already IN the first email. AI makes this cheap. |
| **S** | Custom lead magnet | C or S depending on effort. S when built with real upfront research (browser agents, meticulous report). Best = stack: situation proof pack + creative ideas + competitive teardown in one asset. |
| **S** | Creative ideas pack | Eric Nowoslawski classic. Encode your unique context and what is working in your business into ideas at scale via AI; strongest for founder-led marketing. Ideas must be differentiated vs competitors, not generic. |
| **A** | Dinner / roundtable | Small industries where relationships matter, and mid-market / enterprise. Hang it on an event: scrape the event page, cold-email attendees, use the dinner to share case studies (Keller: 42 meetings from one event). |
| **A** | Lookalike case study | All-time banger; the more specific to them the better. Weakened by overuse unless the logo is exceptional; stack it ("done for 7 HR recruiting firms just like yours"). Turns engagers into buyers. |
| **A** | Benchmark scorecard | S* for data-driven products, especially third-party benchmarks positioned around making more money (e.g. a sequencer benchmark on 4M sends). Not for non-data products. |
| **A** | Free pilot / POC | Polarising; works where the product can prove itself fast, otherwise C/D. |
| **A** | Situation proof pack | Public data proving their pain is live now (Jordan Crawford's "pain-qualified segment"). Signal-led: quantify their pain publicly → pitch becomes contextually relevant, good sales-call context, more likely to lead to revenue. |
| **B** | Competitive teardown | Strong where the buyer lives on competitor monitoring (online businesses: ads, SEO, AI search teardowns of competitors who are winning). |
| **B** | Loom teardown | Keller closed his first 12 clients with it, but time-heavy and must be founder/product-led (you need depth to make the video valuable). Either send a pre-recorded link, or OFFER to record (never claim you recorded when you have not). Short, clear next step. |
| **B** | Guarantee | See §3. |
| **B** | Swag / merch | More a customer play; only cold-viable with genuinely great swag. |
| **B** | Tool credits | PLG: above free trial, because credits feel like they expire → loss aversion drives use. |
| **B** | Free trial | Must-try for PLG (S/A); B-C for everyone else. Reads as work THEY must do, vs a pilot which is work YOU do. |
| **B** | Free community | Strong for education-led products; depends on who is already inside. |
| **B** | ROI calculator | A personal benchmark (they input data, get something useful back); limited use. |
| **C** | Free workshop | Only with an existing audience (viral on LinkedIn + signal-led outbound can lift it to S); otherwise weak. |
| **C** | Early access | Manufactured exclusivity; waitlists and closed betas generate interest, not reliable revenue. |
| **D** | Strategy call + asset | "Who wants a consulting call?" Same failure as the free audit. |
| **D** | Pay for performance | Not effective as a cold offer. |
| **D** | Webinar invite | Outbound only: nobody cares about your webinar; at least a workshop can be in person. Fine on other channels. |
| **D** | Customer intro | Intro to a peer customer as the offer; often abused or faked. |
| **D** | Launch discount | Self-explanatory. |
| **D** | Done-with-you setup | Not cold-ready; a closing tool for warm people stuck on friction (signed up, not paid). |
| **D** | Free audit | "Nobody wants to know what's wrong; they want someone who can fix it." If you can audit from the outside, just send it as a custom lead magnet. **Replace with an opportunity report**: what the opportunity is + how to capture it (opportunity report + action plan, or + creative ideas). |

Cross-cutting rules from the ranking [keller-27]:
- **Research depth decides the tier.** Most offers move up a tier with real per-account research (Firecrawl, Claygent, browser agents); without upfront research, stay with the simpler offers and drop gift card / charity lower (they need to feel researched to work).
- **Humor and speaking their language lift every offer**; putting yourself in their shoes is the multiplier across the whole list.
- Positive framing beats negative: opportunity > audit; "make more money" > "fix what's broken" (consistent with `creator-radar-cold-email.md` "anchor on make money").

## 6. How to apply (gtm-master procedure)
1. Write the current offer in the baseline shape (§1). If it is a description, say so.
2. Run the corrected value equation (§2): name the worldview-alignment facts, cap the dream outcome at what a stranger can believe, attach a mechanism to every speed/ease claim.
3. Decide intro vs full offer by buyer state (§4); for cold lists, build the intro slice and check the 5 rules.
4. Pick the offer TYPE from §5 that fits the product and research capacity; consider stacking an S-tier add-on in the PS (gift card / charity) on top of a low-friction question CTA.
5. **Signal fit check:** the offer must speak to the pain the list's signal implies (situation proof pack logic). If the signal says "hiring X now", the offer must be about that hire, not the whole service catalogue.
6. **Client rules win.** If a client bans value-based CTAs (deliverable promises, free data, samples), the S-tier "promise a deliverable" offers are out for that client; keep only what the rule allows (e.g. the PS add-on question, worldview-aligned problem framing, a specific risk reversal). Never override a client rule with this file.
7. Then frame it with `tasks/reframe-offer.md` and write with `tasks/write-first-email.md`.
