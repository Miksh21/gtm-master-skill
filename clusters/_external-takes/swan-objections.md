## Call scorecards — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/kevin-kd-dorsey/call-scorecards · filed 2026-07-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: objections
Behavior-based scorecards per conversation type (prospecting, discovery, demo, pricing, close, champion navigation) used to score real calls, localize where reps get stuck, and drive chunked practice. gtm-master's objections cluster covers written objection replies only (tasks/handle-objection.md); live-call quality has no canon counterpart.
- 🆕 new-angle: card construction rules: start with the 10-15 most important behaviors, not a fully baked card ("aim small, miss small"; a 40-item card nobody uses is the named failure); pull items from what TOP reps actually do on real calls and build with rep + manager input so the card is adopted, not imposed; every item is an observable behavior scored 1-5 ("got them to agree they have a problem", never "good discovery"); grow the card only after the scoring habit exists.
- 🆕 new-angle: chunking as the diagnostic mechanism: items grouped into the call's natural sections (opener → body → wrap-up; discovery → pitch → close → navigation) so a low score localizes exactly where the rep is stuck, "a rep who pitches well but scores low on discovery has a discovery problem, not a demo problem." Practice is chunked too: one role play per rep per week minimum, drilling ONLY the lowest-scoring section with varied prompts "until they leave the session on a five", never the whole call.
- 🆕 new-angle: prospecting-card specifics: a prospecting call is NOT a qualifying call, "opening with qualifying questions (company size, title, stack) kills the meeting rate"; tone is graded FIRST ("calm, caring, assumptive — the lost-in-the-city ask-for-help energy") because it drives everything after; permission opener ("okay if I ask a couple of quick questions to see if it's even worth talking further?"); no gimmick openers ("do you have 27 seconds", "nobody talks that way, so nobody can deliver it naturally"); leads / illusion-of-choice; the "...and by the way" extra question; wrap-up = pause, "might make sense" close built on the problems just discovered, book within three days, invite accepted on the spot, homework with a reason.
- 🆕 new-angle: closing-card specifics: the first question to ask a rep about any deal is "what problem did they agree they have?", problem agreement is the core of discovery; confirm they are sold BEFORE showing pricing; get agreement that something has to change (that agreement is the first close); ABS-style recommendation with benefits restated inside it (not "you get all these seats" but "the API connection that streamlines your security review, and onboarding that cuts ramp from three months to two"); ask for the money or the concrete next step ("Julie's the CFO, right? When can we get her on a call?") instead of ending the call waiting; navigation section = champion personally sold, decision process mapped, and what matters to the decision-maker captured, a scored miss there is an early warning to go back to the champion BEFORE the decision call.
- 🆕 new-angle: scoring cadence economics: SDRs (2-3 convos/day) and AEs (7-8 demos/month) can score EVERY call; reps self-score, managers score a sample, trends tracked so a 90-day line shows which chunk is dragging; scoring doubles as proactive deal rescue (a scored miss names exactly what to go get before the next call); transcript-scoring rule: one line per item with the quoted moment that earned the score or the absence that cost it, "NEVER invent moments — cite the transcript or mark the item unobserved."

## Sales playbook foundations — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/kevin-kd-dorsey/sales-playbook-foundations · filed 2026-07-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: objections
Playbook skeleton (the five P's) plus a fully drafted People section, "the part almost every playbook is missing." No gtm-master counterpart (enable-sales.md is campaign enablement, not playbook structure).
- 🆕 new-angle: the five P's in a deliberate order: People, Prospect, Problem, Process, Product, "most playbooks start with product and never get to people, which is backwards: people are the ones who execute the plays."
- 🆕 new-angle: the People section's four blocks: (1) Expectations with every target broken into re-derivable math ("$100K per quarter" = $10K average deal x 10 deals at 25% close, inbound covers it if the rep self-sources 5 opportunities) plus promotion AND termination criteria known from day one; (2) Standards of excellence: the org's own virtues ("how you behave") separate from wall values ("what you believe"), each defined with a real example, ending in a signature page, "that signature is what the first hard conversation points back to"; (3) personal goals set at start; (4) stories and records: the legends, comeback stories, last-second deals, failures, one top tip per rep, and the records (fastest ramp, biggest deal), "new people need to identify with those who came before."
- 🆕 new-angle: adoption tests: the remaining P's are built from real calls, real deals, real rep language, never the ideal process nobody runs; never fully outsourced ("outside help is fine, but the leader stays heavily involved or it won't be adopted"); owner + review cadence assigned before shipping; the acceptance test: "could a rep who joins after the leader has left succeed from this document alone?"

## Battlecard — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/ido-goldberg/battlecard · filed 2026-07-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: objections
Creates or updates a competitor battlecard saved into org competitive knowledge. gtm-master handles inbound competitor objections (handle-objection.md "we already have a vendor") but has no battlecard artifact.
- 🆕 new-angle: the six-section canonical format, total under ~600 words, date-stamped ("competitive intel rots fast"): 1. one-line description (what they sell, to whom) · 2. where they win (3-5 genuine strengths, "don't strawman... a battlecard that pretends the competitor has no advantages doesn't get used") · 3. where they lose (3-5 evidenced weaknesses from public reviews, missing features, known complaints) · 4. common objections in "Objection → our response" pairs, each response <30 words · 5. win conditions (specific buyer profiles, deal sizes, technical requirements, regional factors) · 6. where to push in a deal (demo emphasis, pricing posture, proof points).
- 🆕 new-angle: evidence bars: strengths and weaknesses must cite real evidence or be left out; rumored weaknesses labeled ("reported in G2 reviews"); no legally sensitive claims without citation; cheap-signals-first gathering (homepage + pricing headline/CTA/named segment, customers page pattern, LinkedIn posting cadence and hiring areas, traffic order of magnitude, funding events), skip fetches the user already has fresh.
- 🆕 new-angle: the ghost-competitor check: a "competitor" with under 1,000 monthly visitors and no LinkedIn presence gets flagged, "usually means the user is fighting a ghost. Suggest they reassess whether this competitor is worth a battlecard." Existing notes mean update-not-recreate: "preserve what's true; only revise what's stale"; the card is always saved to shared competitive knowledge, "a battlecard that lives only in chat history is wasted."

## ROI business case — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/gal-aga/roi-business-case · filed 2026-10-26
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: objections
- 🆕 new-angle: never show a single ROI number; model worst / average / best on the same root-cause problem and the same buyer baseline. Each scenario shows value created minus total investment over the contract term, giving net ROI and payback, with the arithmetic on the page and rounded conservatively. The worst case must justify the spend on its own; if it does not, the case is not ready and you say so.
- 🆕 new-angle: baselines come from the buyer's own data, never industry averages or round guesses; missing ones are [CONFIRM WITH BUYER] tasks. Any value that does not trace back to the one root-cause problem is cut, because unrelated "value" makes finance distrust the whole page.
- 🆕 new-angle: each scenario's assumptions (improvement rate, timeframe, baseline) stay visible so a reviewer can disagree and re-run the math; invite the buyer to edit the inputs. Working signals: the buyer re-runs the numbers themselves, and finance stops arguing inflation and starts discussing timing.
- 🆕 new-angle: frame the range as impartiality ("even the worst case pays back in X"), which pre-handles the "your numbers are inflated" objection before it is raised; package it as a one-pager the champion can forward to the CFO without the AE in the room.
