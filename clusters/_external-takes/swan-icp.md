## Category-of-one positioning — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/luke-shalom/category-of-one-positioning · filed 2026-07-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: icp
The "Position" step of the Atticus Method for founder/expertise-led businesses that look interchangeable and get price-shopped. gtm-master's icp cluster defines WHO to sell to; it has nothing on positioning language, so this is a new capability adjacent to build-icp.
- 🆕 new-angle: diagnose interchangeability by building a banned-language list: research how the user AND their closest competitors describe themselves (websites, LinkedIn headlines, service pages), list every shared phrase ("we help X do Y with Z"), and ban all of it from the final positioning, "if the user looks like the other 1,000 options on the shelf, buyers stroll past the shop window." Simultaneously bank the user's own raw phrasing word-for-word: "do not sand their words into cleaner marketing copy — the rough, specific way they say it is the asset."
- 🆕 new-angle: the brokenness interview as the non-skippable data step: for EACH industry pain point, capture three answers verbatim, what is broken about your industry right now (with real examples), why do you think it's broken (the cause under the symptom), what do you think the solution is. "NEVER skip the interview and jump to the reframe", skipping it produces "a clever tagline, which is just the old positioning in new words." ICP never invented: if not findable, ask and WAIT.
- 🆕 new-angle: old-way vs new-way reframe built directly from the interview: a two-column contrast (what the old way is / why it quietly fails / what the new way replaces it with) whose test is that it changes what the buyer thinks their problem IS, example move: lead generation reframed from "a service you rent" to "infrastructure you own." Each candidate reframe tested: would the ICP recognize their situation in the old way, and does the new way make every interchangeable competitor look like the old way?
- 🆕 new-angle: process before result: map the user's REAL step-by-step mechanism before ever stating the outcome ("a new way with no visible mechanism is just a claim"), then package it as named proprietary IP whose steps map to how they actually operate, one real proof point per step, "the stack is generic; the data feeding it is proprietary."
- 🆕 new-angle: category language rules: 5-10 candidate names for the problem, the mechanism, and the outcome; keep only phrases short, concrete, and repeatable in a LinkedIn post, a DM, and a sales call WITHOUT explanation ("if a phrase needs a paragraph of explanation, cut it"); never position against a named competitor, always against the old way; final pressure-test = zero shared phrases with the competitor list, every claim traceable to confirmed track record, mechanism before result, ICP feels seen.

## ICP — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/ido-goldberg/icp · filed 2026-07-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: icp
The refine-and-extend verb over saved ICP segments: pull what's actually winning, propose tightenings, save only what the user approves. Complements icp/tasks/build-icp.md (first-time definition + backtest).
- 🆕 new-angle: refinement floors and caps as hard rules: NEVER refine without at least 10 closed deals ("below that, the signal is noise"); propose 3-5 changes maximum ("the user can't act on 8; pick the 3 that matter", more than 5 means the ICP needs full re-discovery, not refinement); never a full rewrite on a single contrarian quarter ("drift is patterned across multiple slices").
- 🆕 new-angle: "no drift" is a valid and honorable output: "if the data says the current ICP is correct, the right output is 'no meaningful drift, the ICP holds.' Inventing changes to look productive is failure mode #1."
- 🆕 new-angle: every proposed change cites its specific win/loss pattern in one line ("tighten size band to 50-300 (current: 50-1000). wins concentrate in 50-300, losses spike above 500"), scanned across five drift classes: boundaries too wide, boundaries too narrow, missing exclusions ("we keep losing in healthcare — should that be out of scope?"), new segments (a wins cluster fitting no current segment), and persona drift (actual signers/champions on won deals diverging from saved personas).
- 🆕 new-angle: personas live INSIDE the ICP verb, refined in the same pass from who actually signed won deals, "never punt personas to a different skill"; missing firmographics on >20% of deal companies get flagged as a partiality caveat rather than enriched away; no CRM = work from the user's named last 5-10 wins/losses, or state that refinement isn't possible yet.
- ✅ supports [icp/tasks/build-icp.md backtest validation + revisit-after-20-deals]: refinement grounded exclusively in closed-deal evidence with win/loss grouping by industry, size band, stage, geo; user approves each change before anything saves; recommendation to re-score the next unscored batch to validate the refinement.

## Competitor positioning — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/alon-goldenberg/competitor-positioning · filed 2026-09-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: icp
This is a recurring teardown of how competitors present themselves (site, copy, pricing, content), producing a messaging matrix, white space and battlecards. The battlecard layout overlaps the existing ido-goldberg battlecard take in swan-objections.md (the honest "where they win" section, the date stamp), and the anti-shared-language idea overlaps category-of-one in swan-icp.md. The points below exclude both.
- 🆕 new-angle: read the site structure before the copy. Map it first (sitemap or crawl, up to ~200 URLs; guessed paths 404). What is missing is itself a positioning signal: no public pricing page points to a sales-led or enterprise motion, and no blog means the company is not competing on content. Subdomains matter too: a `docs.` subdomain signals a developer audience whatever the homepage claims. Structural changes (a pricing page removed, a new `/enterprise` path) "telegraph strategy quarters before the blog admits it".
- 🆕 new-angle: capture your own baseline first, the same way you capture the competitors' (homepage, features, pricing as clean text). The messaging matrix (Tagline · Primary CTA · Value Props · Target Audience · Pricing Model) must include a row for the user's own company, otherwise "the comparison collapses into a list". If a page cannot be extracted, record "page not accessible" and never guess.
- 🆕 new-angle: store copy verbatim or not at all. Taglines, CTAs, value props and differentiation claims are kept as exact text with a source URL, because "'streamline your workflow' and 'ship faster' are different positioning". Each competitor gets a snapshot file whose top sections show the current state and whose History section is append-only (dated diffs, and "no changes detected" entries too), so a later "when did they pivot to enterprise?" can be answered. If a page is inaccessible on a run, keep the prior value marked "not re-verified [date]" rather than blanking it.
- 🆕 new-angle: two further analysis sections. Content gap: topics, formats (benchmarks, ROI calculators, case studies) and audience segments competitors cover and you do not. Positioning white space: unclaimed value props or segments. White space is an absence claim, so it is only credible after a thorough per-competitor pass. Blog reading looks at recurring narrative, target audience (developer, executive or practitioner), whether posts name competitors or argue against the category, which keywords the titles target, and content maturity (original research vs generic how-tos).
- 🆕 new-angle: positioning and business signals are kept in separate files but cross-linked. Funding or hiring notes from competitor-monitoring often explain why messaging shifted (a raise followed by an enterprise pivot). Delta mode is used within 14 days, and it must read in under 2 minutes. Every recommended action names a competitor claim and the counter-move. A battlecard built on a snapshot older than 14 days first triggers a refresh of that one competitor ("a battlecard built on stale messaging gets a rep corrected in a live call"). With more than 4 competitors, group them by category and let the user pick which to cover.
- ✅ supports [icp/tasks/build-icp.md:91]: evidence stays in the source's exact words (there, the customer's words for the value sentence; here, competitor copy quoted verbatim, never paraphrased).

## ICP lookalike expansion — swan-gtm/gtm-skills/skills/amir-baldiga/icp-lookalike-expansion · filed 2026-09-28
Source: https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills · cluster: icp
Lookalike expansion at the person level from a few converted seeds, using a similarity signature you define yourself instead of a vendor feed. gtm-master's lookalike content is at the account-attribute level (industry, size, geo, tech stack).
- ⚠️ contradicts [list-building/knowledge/sales-navigator-guide.md:278]: gtm-master's lookalike steps include "Use 'Similar Companies' feature on company pages" (and creator-radar-icp.md:10 uses "vector-similar companies"). Swan calls trusting a "people similar to this" feed the mediocre version, because nobody can explain why a row is on the list: "When a rep asks 'why him?', there is no answer." Both sides: gtm-master: black-box similarity features are a valid lookalike source; swan: search only on an explicit signature, so every row survives "what specifically makes this person like the seed?"
- ⚠️ contradicts [icp/knowledge/creator-radar-icp.md:10]: seed size. gtm-master's closed-won seed system starts from "export 30-50 won deals", and sales-navigator-guide.md:275 uses the top 10-20 customers. Swan expands from "a handful" of converted leads, or even one good-fit profile. Both sides: gtm-master: you need a statistically meaningful won set before modelling lookalikes; swan: one well-read seed with an explicit title+employer signature is enough to expand from.
- 🆕 new-angle: two distinct searches answer two different questions. Title + employer finds peers inside the same company (buying-committee mapping, expansion within a won account). Title only, optionally narrowed by geography, finds the same role across the market (net-new list). For the market search you must drop the employer filter, or you get nothing.
- 🆕 new-angle: the signature comes from the seed's CURRENT title and employer, not the headline, which is "often aspirational or stale". Search on the short core role: "Chief executive officer" returns results, "CEO & Founder | Investor | Speaker" returns zero. Rule: never pass a full headline as the title filter.
- 🆕 new-angle: recursion depth as a cost dial. Depth 1 searches from the seed only. Depth 2 re-searches from each discovered profile (dozens of lookups), depth 3 is hundreds. Branch on the top ~10 profiles at most and confirm spend before depth 3. Keep a seen-set across the whole run, not per search, and drop the seed from its own results (most endpoints return it).
- 🆕 new-angle: widen or tighten one filter at a time. Stacking exact title + all-skills-match + city returns zero rows, which reads as "no market" when the query is just over-narrowed. Treat an empty result as a signal: loosen in order (drop employer, shorten title, remove geo) and log which loosening produced the hit. Output is a flat list (handle, name, headline, location, source seed), not the recursion tree.
- ✅ supports [list-building/knowledge/abm/account-selection-framework.md:76-82]: lookalikes are built from explicitly identified shared attributes of the best customers, not from gut feel.
