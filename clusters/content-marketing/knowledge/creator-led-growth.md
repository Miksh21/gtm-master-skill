# Creator-Led Growth: Strategy, Sourcing, Motion Choice, 90-Day Program, Measurement

> Source: swan-gtm/gtm-skills (thomas-marcelle) · MIT · imported 2026-07-28 · external content, unreviewed

B2B influencer/creator marketing on LinkedIn as a pipeline channel. Core thesis: never build on follower count; build on audience-ICP overlap × posting expertise × engagement authenticity. Deal pricing, rate math, negotiation, and benchmarks live in `clusters/content-marketing/knowledge/creator-deal-economics.md`. ## The expertise curve (tier model)

Cost is U-shaped across tiers; trust is inverted-U. Strategy is finding where they cross.

| Tier | Followers | CPM | Trust | B2B fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | <2K | low | high | too small to move pipeline |
| Micro | 2K-10K | low | high | good, limited reach |
| **Mid-tier expert** | **10K-80K** | medium | **peak** | **the sweet spot** |
| Macro | 80K-500K | high | falling | broad, diluted audience |
| Celebrity | 500K+ | absurd | low | vanity, no pipeline |

**Mid-tier expert is the default allocation**: practitioners with a real job title in the buyer's field (a RevOps lead posting about attribution, a CFO posting about SaaS spend). Their audience is 60-80% ICP-dense because expertise filters followers. A macro creator's audience is broad by construction. Note the sourcing skill's complementary finding: in the 1K-10K range creators get treated as peers rather than media, which shows up directly in click intent; ten aligned micro-creators typically beat one mega-creator.

## Lock the brief before any creator name

Restate every engagement as WHO + WHAT + PROOF + BUDGET:
- **WHO**: the ICP persona creators must reach (title, industry, geo, size). One persona per campaign.
- **WHAT**: the one action expected (demo, trial, waitlist, category awareness). Different actions need different creator archetypes.
- **PROOF**: what the creator can credibly say (a use case, a metric, a before/after, a personal product test). No proof = no post worth paying for.
- **BUDGET**: the monthly envelope. Budget determines creator mix, never the reverse.

Push back on "get us big influencers" without pipeline rationale: a 200K-follower generalist produces impressions; a 25K-follower practitioner produces meetings. Surface the trade-off before obeying.

## Audience mapping (before influencer mapping)

Start from the buyer, work backwards: list 5-10 accounts/communities where the ICP already spends attention (LinkedIn voices, newsletters, Slack communities, podcasts); note recurring themes that get engagement from ICP-titled commenters; extract 3-4 content angles the ICP demonstrably cares about. These become campaign themes. Prevents the classic failure: picking creators the founder admires instead of creators the buyer follows.

## The 60/30/10 portfolio

- **60% mid-tier experts (10K-80K)**: the pipeline engine. 4-8 creators at 1-2 posts/month. Bar: current or recent operator role in the ICP's function, ICP titles in the comments, posts that teach rather than broadcast.
- **30% micro experts (2K-10K)**: volume and testing layer. 8-15 creators, cheap flat fees, best cost-per-engagement in the market. Test messaging angles here, then scale winners to mid-tier creators. Cap exposure per creator.
- **10% one macro anchor (80K+)**: air cover for a launch moment only, and only above ~€5K/month budget. Below that, reallocate to mid-tier.

Portfolio rules: no creator above 25% of monthly budget (performance variance is high; diversification is the hedge). Prefer 6 mid-tier experts over 1 macro at equal spend: six credible voices repeating a message in the same feed-week creates surround-sound one big post cannot. Expertise is a hard filter, not a bonus: no practitioner credibility in the buyer's domain = excluded at any follower count. A 60/30/10 portfolio needs 12-25 active creators; manual DM-based sourcing caps around 5-10 relationships, so at that scale use a creator marketplace (e.g. Naano for B2B LinkedIn) for sourcing, vetting, flat-fee booking, payment, and per-creator tracking, keeping manual outreach for the bespoke macro anchor.

## Sourcing: five discovery methods

1. **Mine engaged audiences**: export commenters on the last 20 relevant posts in the space; people already engaging the topic.
2. **Targeted topic search**: search narrow problem language ("outbound deliverability"), not category names; note who posts on it consistently.
3. **Second-degree analysis**: study who strong creators engage with; smaller accounts with sharp commentary often outperform bigger names.
4. **Competitor sponsorships**: public sponsored posts reveal creators already proven with the target market.
5. **Marketplaces**: pre-vetted, bookable, filterable by topic and audience.

## Vetting order (before follower count is even considered)

1. **Audience fit**: read the comment sections; what share of commenters carry buyer job titles? Ten on-ICP commenters beat a thousand random impressions.
2. **Engagement quality**: saves, thoughtful replies, reshares from real profiles. Likes are vanity. Pods trade empty praise; comment-section quality is the overlooked check.
3. **Consistency**: roughly 2 posts/week for 6+ months. A one-time viral spike predicts unreliability.
4. **Voice test**: if the creator stopped mentioning brands entirely, would their audience still read them? That is the authority being rented.

Then score candidates 1-5 on four axes: **ICP density** (sample 30 recent commenters; ≥40% ICP titles = 5), **expertise signal** (holds or recently held the role; specifics only a practitioner would know), **engagement authenticity**, **content-product fit** (can the product appear without breaking their voice). Drop anyone ≤2 on ICP density or expertise regardless of reach. A high-reach low-expertise creator is the most expensive mistake in B2B influencer marketing.

A good shortlist: 8-12 creators, each with the ICP share of their commenters (with examples), posting cadence, and the overlap between their recurring topics and the product's problem space. A bad one: profiles ranked by follower count with an engagement-rate column.

## Founder vs employees vs creators (who carries distribution)

The three are layers and stages, not rivals. The one-question fork: **do the people you need to reach already follow your employees?** Yes → advocacy amplifies real relationships. No → creators are how you rent audiences you don't own. Network reference points: advocacy CTR ~1-2% vs ~8-14% on aligned creator posts; CPL €40-80 (advocacy) vs €15-25 (creators).

**Founder-led is the default start, with a ceiling.** A founder posting 3-5×/week on a personal account (3-5× the reach of a company page) can drive 5-25 inbound demos/month, and outbound referencing a founder's post replies at ~40% vs ~5% cold. Saturation arrives around 15-25K followers: engagement-to-reach declines, demo inbound plateaus, and the comment section shifts from buyers to peer founders. Run the founder's math honestly: 8 h/week at €200/h is ~€6,400/month; if that yields ~10 demos, the same budget on creators typically produces more qualified clicks in a fraction of the founder's time, while founder posts keep converting best downstream. Keep the founder posting; stop the founder being the only channel.

When each wins: **founder** for early credibility, high-trust categories, the voice no one can outsource; **employees** for warm-intro enterprise motions, launch moments, recall inside existing networks; **creators** for net-new buyer reach, pipeline economics, verticals where the founder has no standing.

**The 30-45 day transition (founder → creator-augmented).** Week 1: pick the 1-2 verticals where founder reach is thinnest. Week 2: match ~5 creators; brief the narrative arc, not the sales deck. Week 3: stagger creator posts alongside (not replacing) the founder cadence. Week 4: measure per creator, keep the top 3, retire the rest. Creator-driven pipeline typically matches the founder's own within 60 days, from audiences the founder never reached. Never stop founder posting to fund creators: layer, don't swap. Never judge the three motions on the same metric (founder on demo conversion and reply-rate lift, advocacy on recall, creators on cost per qualified lead). The overlooked failure: reading founder saturation as "LinkedIn stopped working" and cutting the channel.

## Brief for authenticity, not control

Provide exactly: one angle (from audience mapping, not three), one proof element, one CTA with its link, and what NOT to say (uncleared claims, competitor bashing). Then let the creator write in their own voice. Posts that read like ads underperform authentic practitioner takes 3-5× on engagement, worse on conversions. If a draft comes back sounding like a press release, send it back: you paid for their voice, not yours. ## Measure, cut, scale (monthly cadence)

1. Per creator: impressions, engagement rate, clicks, qualified conversations, cost per qualified conversation (the real unit of performance; impressions are diagnostics).
2. **Cut the bottom third** on cost per qualified conversation. Data, not sentiment; sunk creator relationships are the silent budget leak.
3. **Double posting frequency on the top third before adding new creators**: a proven creator at 2×/month beats an unproven one at 1×/month. Renewals outperform: a proven creator's second and third posts beat their first.
4. Promote winning micro-expert angles into the mid-tier bucket.
5. Backfill cuts the same week (marketplace liquidity makes the replace-and-compound loop possible).
6. Re-run four-axis scoring quarterly; audiences and expertise decay.

Expect concentration: the top third of creators generates the majority of qualified clicks. The program's job is finding them fast and doubling down.

## The 90-day program arc

**Days 1-30, foundation**: define ICP and core message precisely (week 1); source 8-12 creators at 70-90% audience-ICP fit (week 2); set commercial terms and wire tracking, qualified click = tracked + ≥30 s on-site (week 3); brief with angles and proof points, never scripts (week 4). Milestone: briefed cohort, live tracking.

**Days 31-60, launch-measure-cut**: stagger posts over two weeks so early angle signal shapes the rest; read qualified-click data per creator, ignore vanity metrics; by week 8 cut the bottom third and redeploy budget to top performers. Milestone: per-creator leaderboard, budget reallocated.

**Days 61-90, systematize**: document what won (creator profiles, angles, post structures); build the pipeline forecast from your own cost-per-qualified-click history; launch a second cohort matched to the proven winner profile. Milestone: a playbook another operator could run, a forecast finance accepts, a repeatable process.

The strategy is not done until it exists as an artifact: creator portfolio (names or sourcing criteria), budget table, brief template, 90-day calendar. Never end on advice.

## ROI measurement (the CFO view)

Creator content converts on a considered timeline; direct-response measurement habits systematically undercount it. Three attribution fixes that typically recover 2-3× in reported ROI without changing spend:
- **Window: 14-30 days, not 7.** Median time-to-MQL on a creator-sourced click runs 9-14 days (vs 2-4 for ads); buyers read, save, consult colleagues, then convert. Never judge creator spend on 7-day last-click.
- **View-through**: lift studies show 8-15% incremental traffic that never credits to the post. Measure it separately and labeled, or accept undercounting.
- **Separation**: keep creator-sourced clicks out of the organic-LinkedIn bucket. Blending flatters organic and buries the channel that earned the clicks.

Funnel: spend → qualified clicks (tracked + ≥30 s on-site) → MQL → SQL → opportunities → closed-won. Five-row CFO dashboard: total spend, qualified clicks, effective CPL, MQL→SQL→pipeline conversion, payback period. Payback = spend ÷ monthly gross-margin contribution of the closed-won it produced (a €5,000 quarter yielding €60,000 ARR at 80% margin pays back in ~1.25 months; state it that way, not as abstract ROAS).

Week-one leading indicators, before pipeline exists: CTR ≥ 8%, landing-page bounce < 55%, lead-form completion ≥ 10% of clicks. Missing all three predicts a weak cohort: fix the angle or audience before spending more. Network reference points: creator-post CTR ~8-14% vs under 1% for sponsored content; CPL ~€15-25 mid-market vs €55-90 ads; MQL→SQL ~35-45%.

## Anti-patterns

- Selecting by follower count and calling engagement rate "vetting".
- One macro creator absorbing the budget because "one big post will do it".
- CPM or affiliate-only deals pushing creators toward clickbait and pod engagement.
- Scripted briefs that turn a practitioner into a billboard.
- Paying a generalist "B2B influencer" with no operating history in the buyer's function.
- Measuring in impressions while sales sees zero sourced conversations.
- Adding new creators while proven ones post once a month.
- Skipping the week-8 cut (sentiment keeps underperformers funded).
- Reporting 7-day last-click numbers in an ads template and concluding creators "don't perform".
