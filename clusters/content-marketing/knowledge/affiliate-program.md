# B2B Affiliate Program Playbook

> Source: swan-gtm/gtm-skills (victor-moen) · MIT · imported 2026-07-28 · external content, unreviewed

Building or fixing a B2B SaaS affiliate program. Operating thesis: **affiliates aren't links, they're people.** A program with 1,000+ signed-up affiliates can still drive nothing; winning programs win on recruitment quality and activation, not signup volume. When diagnosing an existing program, look at partner-mix quality and activation rate before touching commission rates: the problem is almost never fixed by paying more to the wrong partners.

## 1. Ideal partner personas (IPPs)

Reverse-engineer the buyer journey: where do ideal customers hang out online, and who influences them there? Typical IPPs: micro-influencers on LinkedIn and YouTube, agencies and consultancies serving the ICP, niche communities, newsletters, content and review sites, high-performing partners of competitors.

**Ignore coupon sites.** They attract deal-hunters, not high-value buyers, and take credit for sales that would have happened anyway.

## 2. Commission structure from unit economics

Derive from the Allowable CAC formula, never from "what competitors pay":

```
Allowable CAC = LTV × (1 / target LTV:CAC ratio)
```

Example: $8,000 LTV at a 4:1 target = $2,000 allowable CAC. Then pick the model to fit the motion:
- Strong unit economics → aggressive flat CPA, even 100-200% of first-month revenue.
- Freemium with slow conversion → tiered revshare (capped at 12 months) plus cost-per-free-signup for trusted content partners, rewarding partners early instead of making them wait.
- One flat tier for everyone under-rewards the partners who matter.

## 3. Cookie window

Longer than the conversion cycle, **90 days minimum**. A window shorter than the sales cycle silently robs partners of credit and kills trust.

## 4. Stack and gating

Run on a real partner platform (PartnerStack, impact.com, FirstPromoter, Dub) for clean attribution and automatic payouts; less admin means more time for recruitment and activation. **Gate with an application form**: accept only brand-aligned partners who can drive volume, and include a "what partner type are you?" question so coupon sites are rejected at the door. If it's not a "heck yeah," it's a "heck no."

## 5. Recruitment engine (the lifeblood)

Three motions in parallel:
- **Inbound**: make applying easy, never rely on it alone; the best partners aren't looking.
- **Large-scale outbound**: build partner lists matching the IPPs, human-review every partner before outreach, enrich contacts, pull each partner's recent content for genuinely personalized openers, run omni-channel (email + LinkedIn) sequences.
- **High-touch super-affiliates**: partners capable of $50K+/month need a warm intro and a relationship, not a sequence.

Speed to lead is decisive: respond to applications as fast as to demo requests.

## 6. Onboarding (activation from <5% to 30-40%)

Four emails on join: (1) welcome + time-sensitive first-sale bonus + commission breakdown + referral link; (2) proven content formats + asset library + clear CTA; (3) the path to first sale, the fastest ways to win; (4) benefits recap + 1-1 support from a named partner manager. Check in at day 14 and day 30 if no activity. Done well, this moves activation from the industry's under-5% toward 30-40% within 30 days.

## 7. Launch challenge

Activate with a program-launch (or relaunch) challenge: a grand prize worth talking about (in-person mastermind, cash) plus a participation prize anyone can win (a badge and merch for a single sale). Everybody should be able to win something; that builds momentum and trust. Time-boxed double-commission periods work the same way: not a cost, an activation budget. You're paying to activate lead-getters who bring customers for years.

## 8. Communication and the top 20%

Three pillars: campaigns (first-sale challenges, seasonal promos), a bi-weekly newsletter segmented by partner type and activity, 1-1 development with top performers and rising stars. The top 20% of affiliates produce 80% of revenue; treat them accordingly: shared Slack channel, monthly calls, first access to product updates, gifts. Running an affiliate program is a human-connection job with a system behind it.

## 9. Audit, experiment, remove fraud

Full partner audit twice a year: tag partners by type, potential, activity; reactivate high-potential dormant partners; cut the bottom tier. Remove fraudulent partners aggressively: coupon and brand-bidding partners show "revenue" on paper while intercepting sales already happening. Run experiments on commissions, incentives, assets, partner types, geos, verticals; identify which partner types outperform and recruit more of them.

## Rules

- MUST derive commission from Allowable CAC math, never from competitor rates.
- MUST gate the program; NEVER accept coupon or brand-bidding partners; purge on sight in audits.
- MUST human-review every partner before outreach: recruitment quality over volume, always.
- NEVER measure by signups; measure activated partners and partner-sourced revenue.
- NEVER let the cookie window undercut the sales cycle.

## What good looks like

Small and active, not large and dead: a gated partner base matching the IPPs, activation well above the sub-5% norm, the top 20% on a first-name basis with the team, revenue attributable to partners who created demand rather than intercepted it. Failing-program tells: signup counts celebrated as a KPI, coupon sites in the list, a flat commission tier, nobody owning partner relationships.
