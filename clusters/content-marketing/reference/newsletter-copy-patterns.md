# Newsletter Copy Patterns: Subject Lines, Optimizer Rules, Pain Specificity, Email Templates, Idea Bank

> Source: swan-gtm/gtm-skills (daniel-bustamante) · MIT · imported 2026-07-28 · external content, unreviewed

Reference library for newsletter and list-email copy. This is warm-audience copy (subscribers who opted in), not cold outbound: for cold email subject lines use `clusters/cold-email/tasks/write-subject-lines.md` (different rules: under 6 words, curiosity over clarity).

## Subject lines for newsletter/list emails

Style rules: under 50 characters where possible (mobile cutoff; the optimizer uses 42 as the stricter bar). Specific numbers over vague claims. Lowercase often outperforms Title Case. Questions outperform statements for cold audiences. Specificity beats cleverness. One idea per line. Emojis sparingly, matching the sender's usual style. Banned words: "Unlock", "Discover", "Revolutionary", "Game-changing", "Don't miss". A good batch is diverse: different frameworks, not five variations of one hook, and never clickbait the body doesn't deliver.

Match framework category to email intent:

| Email intent | Best categories |
|---|---|
| Sales/promo | Urgency, FOMO, Results, Value |
| Educational | Curiosity, Quick Win, Contrarian |
| Story-based | Story, Pain Point, Transformation |
| Authority-building | Authority, Social Proof, Results |
| Challenge/launch | Action, Empowerment, Urgency |
| Newsletter/content | Curiosity, Value, Quick Win, Trend |

### Preview text

Complement, never repeat, the subject line. Add the specificity or intrigue the subject lacks. 40-90 characters (shows fully on mobile). Build a 1-2 punch:

| Subject style | Preview approach |
|---|---|
| Question | Answer hint or amplify stakes |
| Statement | Add proof or specificity |
| Curiosity gap | Partial reveal or benefit |
| Urgency | Reinforce deadline or scarcity |
| Number-based | Expand on what's included |

## The 10 email-copy optimizer rules

Apply to any list-email draft. Be specific ("change X to Y because Z"), preserve the author's voice, prioritize subject line and intro before formatting, and always deliver the full rewritten email, not just feedback.

1. **10+ subject line variations, all under 42 characters.** The email doesn't matter if nobody opens it. Different psychological angles across the batch.
2. **Intro written like a LinkedIn hook.** Skip greetings; the first 1-2 lines must force the next line. Bad: "Hope you're having a great week. I've been thinking..." Good: "I make a living writing emails for 7, 8, and 9 figure founders. Here are 10 tips..."
3. **Speak to one reader.** Kill crowd-speak ("Hey everyone" → "Hey"). Aim for 80%+ second person ("you") vs first/third person.
4. **Edit for mobile.** Break any 2-3 sentence paragraph into 1-sentence paragraphs with line breaks; blocky desktop paragraphs overwhelm on a phone.
5. **Do copywork on the greats** (practice habit, not a per-draft action).
6. **Read it aloud.** Flag anything clunky, non-flowing, or boring.
7. **Sub-heads for skimmability.** Listicles: one per item. Non-listicles: 3-5 minimum. Promote the key sentences that drive points home into sub-heads.
8. **Always include a PS** (one of the most-read sections). Value emails with no body CTA → soft contextual CTA in PS. Sales emails → handle a common objection or reinforce urgency/deadline in PS.
9. **Contextual CTAs.** Two levers: placement (the most relevant section) and phrasing (congruent with surrounding copy), never bolted on.
10. **Blocky paragraphs → bullets.** Max 1-2 bulleted lists per section, 1-2 intro sentences before each list, short bullets.

## Pain Is The Pitch (emotional specificity rewrite)

Hormozi's principle: articulate a prospect's pain accurately enough and they infer "this person couldn't know my exact experience unless they've lived it or solved it for many people." Trust + acute pain = high purchase likelihood.

Two tactics:
1. **Timing: sell at the point of greatest deprivation**, not satisfaction. Ask someone who just ate a steak if they want another steak and they say no. Emails addressing an ACTIVE pain convert; benefit lists don't.
2. **Specificity: pain happens in moments.** "I was overweight" is forgettable; "I wore a cover-up at the beach and avoided photos" is visceral. Capture the MOMENT.

Rewrite process: (1) scan the draft for generic phrases ("save money", "improve your confidence", "struggling with X", "get better results", "next level"); (2) per vague section ask: what do they hope to achieve, when is the pain most intense, what specific situation do they experience; (3) add sensory, tangible detail. Progression example: "Struggling with email marketing?" → "Staring at a blank screen for 2 hours trying to write one email" → "It's 11 PM. You've been staring at a blank Google Doc for 2 hours. Your eyes hurt. Your coffee's cold. And you still haven't written a single word of tomorrow's email."

Per vague section produce 3 rewrites (pain-focused, different angle, sensory/moment-driven), keep the author's voice, then output the full rewritten email. Quality bar: the reader thinks "that's exactly what happens to me."

## Email type templates

### Weekly roundup (curated digest)

Formats: link roundup, lesson roundup, tool roundup, own-content roundup, hybrid. The differentiator vs a boring link dump: **your commentary on every item**, never just a description. Structure: intro (2-3 sentences, sets the week's tone, previews contents) → 5-7 items, each as [emoji] [title or one-line hook] + 2-3 sentences of YOUR angle + link, leading with the strongest item and alternating types → outro/CTA (reply question, next-week tease, soft offer bridge, or personal note) → 3 subject options (curiosity / value / casual). 400-600 words, scannable in 2 minutes, items connect to a theme, reads like a person, not an aggregator.

### "I made this for you" (resource announcement)

Frames a new free resource as a gift, not a pitch; routinely 2-3× the CTR of standard newsletters. 5 parts, 300-500 words:
1. **Backstory (≥30% of the email)**: why you built it. Angles: the pattern you noticed ("I keep getting the same question"), the problem you solved for yourself, the frustration that triggered it, the conversation that inspired it, the result that proved it. 3-5 sentences.
2. **The reveal**: "So I put together [Name], a [format] that [primary benefit]." One clean sentence, then briefly what makes it different.
3. **Value stack**: 3-5 concrete bullets. Bad: "strategies to grow your list". Good: "the exact 3-question survey I use on my thank-you page to segment subscribers (templates included)". The one place bullets belong.
4. **CTA**: one action, one link ("Grab it here", reply trigger "Reply SEND IT", or a LinkedIn comment-giveaway bridge). Repeat once at the end for skimmers. Never split attention across links.
5. **PS**: social proof, urgency ("free for now, might join [paid offer] later"), a subtle paid-offer bridge ("this is one of the templates inside [offer]"), or a personal note ("I spent [X hours] on this").

### LinkedIn post → newsletter issue (repurposing bridge)

Your best posts are proven: hook worked, idea resonated. Expand into email without bloating. Inputs: the post, what to expand ("add a mini case study to tip #2"), optional real context/anecdotes/stats. **Never fabricate examples, stats, or stories**; flag gaps instead: "[Note: I'd love a mini case study here, do you have a client example?]".

Structure: subject (2 options, under 42 chars, sentence case, curiosity gap) → opening (reintroduce the post's core message, brief personal context, set expectations, 2-4 short paragraphs) → body (expand with subheads; 80% one-sentence paragraphs; 1-2 bullet lists; no emojis; deepen via mini case study, before/after, step-by-step, pro tip, or a personal stat; suggest image placements, never insert) → soft CTA (reply invite, question, resource pointer) → optional PS (1-2 lines: tease next issue, objection, social proof). 500-600 words, max 800.

For the reverse direction and general repurposing mechanics see `clusters/linkedin-organic/content/repurposer.md` (the linkedin-organic cluster owns LinkedIn-side strategy).

## The 52-format idea bank (6 categories)

Use for annual planning (one per week) or any "what do I write" moment. Batch mix for 10 ideas: educational 2-3, story 2, contrarian 1-2, curated 1, engagement 1, promotional 1. Every idea must be specific enough to draft immediately, with a built-in angle.

**Educational (10):** 5-Step Process (master a skill, with pitfalls) · Top 7 Myths (data-backed corrections) · $X-to-$Y Case Study (3 implementable takeaways) · Power Stack (the exact 5 tools + setup tips) · Ask the Expert (3 counterintuitive strategies) · Deep Dive (complete breakdown of one technique) · Head-to-Head (A vs B, pros/cons, who fits which) · First 30 Days (beginner's daily actions; why 80% quit) · Breaking the Ceiling (past a specific plateau) · Emerging Opportunity (under-the-radar trend + 3-step plan)

**Inspirational (10):** Zero to Hero (3 pivotal decisions) · Transformation Blueprint (before/after with metrics) · Against All Odds (obstacle + mindset shift) · Year Ahead Vision (3 developments + preparations) · Origin Story (personal why) · Member Spotlight (community result + exact implementation) · Million-Dollar Mistake (the costly error and its lesson) · Production Line (behind-the-scenes 7-step process) · Personal Rules (3 unconventional guidelines + worksheet) · Milestone (anniversary lessons)

**Engagement (10):** Crossroads Decision (are you making the crucial mistake) · Unpopular Opinion (why common advice hurts) · Next 12 Months prediction (what becomes obsolete) · You Spoke We Listened (survey results + solutions) · 7-Day Challenge (daily 15-min actions + tracker) · Sacred Cow Tipping (respected belief holding you back) · Part 1 of 3 open loop · What Would You Do scenario (option A vs B) · Your Next Breakthrough (reply for a personalized resource) · Mailbag (5 most-asked questions incl. the one nobody dares ask)

**Practical value (10):** Plug-and-Play Template (the exact doc that got a result) · Pre-Launch Checklist (15-point inspection) · 1-Hour Reclaim (5 time-saving methods) · Quick Fix (6 common issues, <15-min solutions) · Go/No-Go Decision (5 questions before investing) · Real Numbers ROI (calculator included) · Seasonal Prep (8 tasks, 30 days before the event) · Set It and Forget It (automation walkthrough, screenshots) · Lunch Break Quick Wins (5 sub-30-min improvements) · 12-Week Sprint (quarterly planning method + template)

**Trust-building (10):** Open Kimono (actual metrics incl. the failure) · $XX,XXX Mistake analysis (cost + 3-step prevention) · Buyer Beware (spot the industry scam) · Data Deep Dive (findings contradicting belief) · Contrarian Approach (the popular practice we avoid and why) · Industry Secrets (3 things pros never discuss) · Success Path (5 phases of the customer journey) · What Actually Works (our method vs the commonly taught one) · Receipts (7 diverse client results) · North Star (3 principles we keep even when they cost us)

**Promotional, value-first (2):** Painful Truth (problem-agitate-solve: the cost, the warning signs, the root-cause solution) · Missing Piece (bridge from the current struggle to the outcome, with a clear path forward)
