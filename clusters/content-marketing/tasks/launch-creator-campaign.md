# Task: Launch a LinkedIn Creator Campaign (Brief → Creators → Posts → Leaderboard)

> Source: swan-gtm/gtm-skills (thomas-marcelle, maxwell-nimmo) · MIT · imported 2026-07-28 · external content, unreviewed

Recipe for taking a B2B creator campaign from zero to live in ~30 days with per-creator tracking, including the per-creator brief and the sponsored-post copy anatomy. This builds a channel, not a one-off media buy.

## When to use this

User wants to launch or test creator/influencer marketing, run sponsored posts, brief a creator, draft or review a sponsored post, or fix a campaign with bad CTR. For full-program strategy (portfolio, budget allocation, founder-vs-creators) load the knowledge files first.

## Inputs required

- **ICP, narrow**: role, company size, vertical, the exact problem ("RevOps leads at 50-200-person SaaS companies drowning in manual reporting", never "B2B SaaS marketers")
- **ONE conversion event**: trial, demo, or lead magnet. Picked before any creator is sourced
- **Proof element**: a use case, metric, before/after, or product test access the creator can credibly use
- **Budget envelope** and campaign window
- Tracking capability: UTM links + on-site time measurement (qualified click = tracked AND ≥30 s on-site)
- For drafting/reviewing a post: the creator, their niche and recent posts, and the brief

## Principles (non-negotiable)

- **Audience-ICP fit over follower count, always**: a creator with 4,000 on-target followers beats one with 80,000 mixed
- **Never script the post; brief the angle.** The creator's voice is the asset being paid for; brand-rewrites reliably halve CTR
- **One tracked CTA** per post; single specific CTAs convert 2-3× better than generic or multiple
- **Flat fee per post** for a first campaign; judge on cost per qualified lead (network median ~€18 vs €55-90 LinkedIn Ads CPL)
- **Per-creator tracking, never campaign-level impressions**
- **3-5 creators to start**: enough to compare, few enough to manage
- One brief per creator; two creators on the same campaign get different angles and different examples

## Process

**Week 1, define.** Lock WHO + WHAT + PROOF + BUDGET. Map where the ICP already spends attention and extract 3-4 content angles that demonstrably engage ICP-titled commenters.

**Week 2, source and vet.** Use the five discovery methods (mine commenters, narrow topic search, second-degree analysis, competitor sponsorships, marketplaces). Vet in order: audience fit (read the actual comment sections for buyer titles) → engagement quality (saves and substantive replies, not likes) → consistency (~2 posts/week for 6+ months) → voice test. Score 1-5 on ICP density, expertise, engagement authenticity, content-product fit; drop ≤2 on the first two. Shortlist 3-5 with evidence per creator.

**Week 3, brief, price, go live.** Price from the rate bands or the weighted-engagement model (see deal-economics knowledge). Write one brief per creator, roughly two pages, 4 parts:

1. **Messaging direction**: the one thing this post should land, for THIS creator
2. **Campaign guidelines**: brand rules, tone, exact product naming, what the client does and doesn't want said
3. **Dos and don'ts**: framing guidance, angles to avoid (only factual and legal guardrails, never messaging mandates)
4. **Content examples**: 3 of the creator's OWN recent high-performers (read them to learn the voice), never generic brand posts

Brief mechanics: fixed go-live date (not a window), draft-due and approval dates ahead of it; tighten the schedule, never the creative. Standard product → open brief. Technical/regulated product (fintech, identity, dev tools, health) → attach a one-pager and explicit factual guardrails (what to call the product, claims to avoid); under-specified technical briefs produce confidently wrong content. A creator should be able to start from the headings and bold text alone. Stagger publishing across the week so early signal shapes later posts.

**Week 4, measure.** Per creator: clicks, qualified clicks, trials/demos, downstream conversion. Ignore impressions. Expect concentration (1-2 creators drive most qualified traffic). Renew winners instead of sourcing fresh; a proven creator's second and third posts outperform their first. Build the leaderboard.

### Drafting or reviewing the sponsored post

Four layers; dropping any one costs 60-80% of CTR:

1. **Hook (line 1)**: concrete, specific, surprising; the feed truncates at ~210 characters. Works: "I cut our SDR team's prospecting time by 6 hours per week." Fails: rhetorical questions, generic claims, brand-name openers.
2. **Story (lines 2-8)**: the lived experience, with the four credibility signals: time-of-day detail ("every Monday at 9am"), specific numbers ("47 stale leads"), a failed attempt before the fix (~2× credibility), internal stakes ("my CEO asked why quota attainment dropped"). All four beat one or two by 30-60%.
3. **Resolution (lines 9-12)**: the product in 1-2 sentences with ONE measured outcome ("20 minutes instead of 3 hours, reply rate 4% → 11%"), never a feature list.
4. **CTA (final line)**: one tracked action naming the specific deliverable ("if you want the exact dashboard config I'm running, I wrote it up: [link]").

Format: 800-1,400 characters, 4-6 paragraph breaks. Under 600 reads as an ad; over 1,800 loses readers before the resolution; dense walls drop completion ~40%.

Anti-patterns by damage order: (1) inauthenticity, the creator has never posted about the category; (2) ad-shaped opener; (3) feature-list resolution; (4) multiple CTAs; (5) brand-voice rewrites. Three or more → expect under 1% CTR. The bar: the post could run unsponsored and still earn its engagement.

## Output format

```
CAMPAIGN: [product] × [ICP]
Conversion event: [one]  ·  Budget: [ ]  ·  Window: [ ]
ANGLES (from audience mapping): 1-3
SHORTLIST: creator · ICP-density evidence · cadence · topic overlap · score (4 axes) · proposed fee
BRIEFS: one per creator (4-part structure above, with dates)
TRACKING: per-creator UTM links + qualified-click definition
WEEK-4 LEADERBOARD: creator · qualified clicks · CPL · conversion · renew? (Y/N)
```

## Pass criteria

- One conversion event chosen before sourcing started
- Every shortlisted creator has comment-section evidence, not just follower/engagement numbers
- Briefs contain zero scripted sentences; examples are the creator's own posts
- Every post has exactly one tracked CTA and all four layers
- Reporting is per-creator qualified clicks and CPL; impressions appear nowhere as a result

## Common failures

- **"Get us big influencers"**: surface the impressions-vs-meetings trade-off; a 25K practitioner produces meetings
- **Draft comes back sounding like a press release**: send it back; you paid for their voice
- **CTR under expectations**: check anti-patterns in order (category authenticity first), then the hook against the 210-character rule
- **Judging results at day 7**: creator clicks convert on a 9-14 day median; use a 14-30 day window
- **Buying five one-off posts and starting over next quarter**: renew winners; treat creators as a channel being built

## What to do after

- Week-one health gates: CTR ≥ 8%, LP bounce < 55%, form completion ≥ 10% of clicks; miss all three → fix angle or audience before more spend
